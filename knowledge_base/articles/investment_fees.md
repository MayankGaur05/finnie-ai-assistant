# How Investment Fees Affect Returns

## Summary

Investment products and services can have fees and expenses. These costs reduce the money that remains invested and can therefore reduce the return an investor keeps over time.

- Costs may include ongoing account or advisory fees, fund operating expenses, transaction charges, and sales loads. Which costs apply depends on the product, service, share class, and account.
- A fund's expense ratio expresses annual operating expenses as a percentage of the fund's average assets. The fund prospectus and fee table describe relevant fund charges; other account or transaction costs may be separate.
- A fee that looks small as a percentage can add up over a long period, especially when it is charged repeatedly on an investment balance.
- When comparing investments or services, review total costs and what services are included. A lower fee alone does not establish that an investment is suitable or will perform better.
- Returns are uncertain, and fees are only one factor to consider alongside risk, time horizon, diversification, and the investor's goals.

## Limitation

This article provides general education, not individualized financial, legal, tax, or investment advice. Fee names, disclosures, and protections vary across products and jurisdictions. Check current documents for a specific investment or service.
