# Stocks and Bonds: Basic Differences

## Summary

Stocks and bonds are different ways investors can provide money to an organization. A stock generally represents an ownership interest in a company. A bond is a debt security: the investor lends money to an issuer, which promises interest payments and repayment of principal under the bond's terms.

- A shareholder may benefit if a company grows or pays dividends, but the stock price can fall and the investor may lose some or all of the amount invested.
- A bondholder is a creditor of the issuer. The issuer's ability to make payments depends on its financial condition, and a bond can lose value before maturity.
- Bonds have risks, including default risk, interest-rate risk, inflation risk, and liquidity risk. If interest rates rise, the market value of an existing bond may fall; selling before maturity can result in a gain or loss.
- Stocks and bonds can behave differently, but holding both does not guarantee a profit or prevent losses. The risk of an investment depends on its features and the issuer, as well as the investor's circumstances.
- Investment choices should be considered in light of goals, time horizon, ability to handle losses, fees, and diversification. These are educational factors to understand, not a recommendation to buy a particular security.
