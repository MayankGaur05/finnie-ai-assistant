# Direct Taxes in India

## Summary

A direct tax is generally imposed directly on a person or entity under a tax law. The person or entity with the legal obligation is responsible for reporting and paying it, subject to that law's rules. This differs from many indirect taxes, which are associated with a transaction and may be collected by a seller or other intermediary.

- Income tax on individuals and businesses is a common example of a direct tax.
- Other taxes may be classified as direct taxes when they are imposed directly on a person, entity, income, property, or other tax base. The precise classification depends on the relevant law.
- Direct-tax duties can include registration, reporting income, filing returns, paying tax, and meeting record-keeping requirements. Which duties apply depends on the taxpayer, income, tax period, and current law.
- A tax system may use rates, thresholds, deductions, or other provisions to determine tax due. These details change and should be checked for the applicable tax year rather than inferred from a general definition.
- Direct taxes are distinct from indirect taxes such as GST, which is connected with a supply or transaction. A business may collect an indirect tax, while the customer may bear some or all of its cost through the price.

## India's income-tax law depends on the tax period

India's Income-tax Act, 2025 took effect on April 1, 2026, replacing the Income-tax Act, 1961 for tax years beginning on or after that date. The 1961 Act continues to govern relevant earlier tax years and transitional matters. As a result, the terminology, provisions, and filing requirements to use depend on the period involved.

## Limitation

This article gives a general definition, not tax, legal, or accounting advice. It does not determine whether a particular person must file a return or how much tax is due. Use the current Income Tax Department guidance and rules for the relevant tax period, or consult a qualified tax professional.
