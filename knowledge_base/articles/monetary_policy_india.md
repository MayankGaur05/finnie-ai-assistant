# Monetary Policy in India

## Summary

Monetary policy is the use of central-bank tools to influence financial conditions, including interest rates, liquidity, and credit. In India, the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) is responsible for conducting monetary policy under the RBI Act. The Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) determines the policy rate within the statutory framework.

- Monetary policy aims to support price stability while keeping economic growth in view. The objectives and operating framework are set by law and official policy.
- A policy-rate change can influence the rates banks offer on deposits and charge on loans, but the effect may take time and may differ across lenders and borrowers.
- Changes in borrowing costs and credit availability can affect household spending, business investment, demand, inflation, and economic activity. These effects are indirect and not guaranteed.
- The RBI may use liquidity-management operations and other instruments as part of the operating framework. The exact tools and their use can change with conditions.
- The MPC publishes policy decisions and related information. Such announcements describe the committee's assessment and choices at that time; they are not guarantees about future inflation, rates, or economic growth.
- Monetary policy is different from **fiscal policy**. Fiscal policy concerns government revenue, spending, and borrowing, while monetary policy is conducted by the RBI under its mandate.

## Following monetary-policy information

For current decisions, read the latest RBI policy resolution, statement, and meeting material. Older articles may contain obsolete policy rates, reserve ratios, inflation targets, meeting schedules, or committee details. This summary intentionally omits current numeric settings because they can change.
