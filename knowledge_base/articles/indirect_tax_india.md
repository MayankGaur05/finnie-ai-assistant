# Indirect Taxes in India

## Summary

An indirect tax is charged in connection with the supply or use of goods and services rather than being based directly on a person's income. A business may collect a tax during a transaction and remit it to the government. The tax cost may be reflected in the price paid by a customer, although who legally pays and who ultimately bears the economic cost can depend on the tax rules and the transaction.

- **Goods and Services Tax (GST)** applies to supplies of goods and services under the GST laws. It is designed as a consumption tax, with mechanisms that can allow eligible tax paid earlier in a supply chain to be credited against tax due later.
- **Customs duties** can apply to goods imported into or exported from India. The applicable treatment depends on the goods and current customs rules.
- **Excise duties** apply to specified goods under applicable central or state laws. The goods covered and relevant procedures depend on current legislation.
- Indirect taxes can affect the final price of a purchase. The amount and the party responsible for collecting or remitting it depend on the product, supply, location, and applicable rules.
- GST concepts include Central GST and State GST for qualifying intra-state supplies, and Integrated GST for qualifying inter-state supplies. The place-of-supply rules determine which treatment applies.
- Input tax credit may reduce tax-on-tax effects for eligible registered businesses when statutory conditions and documentation requirements are met. It is not automatically available for every purchase or taxpayer.

## Indirect tax and direct tax

The distinction is about how a tax is imposed and collected. A direct tax is generally charged directly on a taxpayer, such as tax on income. An indirect tax is associated with a transaction or supply and may be collected by an intermediary. The legal liability and the economic burden do not always fall on the same party.
