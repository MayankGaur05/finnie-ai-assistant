# Tax Deductions and Tax Credits in India

## Summary

Tax deductions and tax credits can both affect a person's tax bill, but they work at different stages of a tax calculation. The rules determine who can claim them, what documentation is needed, and which tax year or regime they apply to.

- A **deduction** reduces the amount of income used to calculate tax, when the law allows it. It does not usually reduce tax payable by the same amount as the deduction.
- A **tax credit** is generally applied against tax calculated, reducing the tax liability directly when the law provides for it. A credit is not the same as a deduction from income.
- An **exemption** generally excludes specified income or a specified amount from a particular tax calculation under applicable conditions. Its effect depends on the law.
- Eligibility can depend on factors such as the type of taxpayer, income, payment, supporting records, tax regime, and tax period. A payment or investment does not automatically qualify for a tax benefit.
- Some deductions or credits may be limited, unavailable under a selected regime, or subject to conditions. Do not assume that a deduction mentioned in an older article is still available or applies to a particular taxpayer.
- India's Income-tax Act, 2025 took effect on April 1, 2026 for tax years beginning on or after that date. Earlier tax years and transitional cases may remain governed by the Income-tax Act, 1961 and relevant transition provisions.

## A simple distinction

Suppose a tax calculation starts with taxable income and then applies a tax rate. A deduction can reduce the income used in that calculation. A credit, if available, is applied later against the calculated tax. The actual calculation may include multiple rules, rates, limits, or adjustments, so this distinction is only a basic explanation.
