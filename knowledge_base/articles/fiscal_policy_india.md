# Fiscal Policy in India

## Summary

Fiscal policy refers to government decisions about public revenue, spending, and borrowing. These decisions can influence demand in the economy, the delivery of public services, investment, employment, and the government's financial position. Union and state governments make budgets and fiscal decisions within their respective legal responsibilities.

- **Revenue** includes taxes and other receipts. **Expenditure** funds public services, transfers, infrastructure, and other programmes.
- Governments may borrow when planned spending exceeds available receipts. Borrowing adds to public liabilities and creates future interest and repayment obligations.
- An **expansionary** fiscal approach may increase spending or reduce some taxes to support demand; a **contractionary** approach may reduce spending or increase revenue to restrain demand or improve the fiscal balance. The actual effect depends on design, timing, financing, and economic conditions.
- A budget is a plan and set of estimates. Actual receipts and spending can differ from budget estimates.
- Deficit and surplus measures describe relationships between specified receipts and expenditures. A deficit alone does not show whether policy is expansionary or contractionary; changes in policy and economic conditions matter.
- Fiscal policy can complement or interact with monetary policy, but the two are set through different institutions and tools. Fiscal decisions are made by governments; monetary policy in India is conducted by the RBI under its statutory framework.
- Fiscal measures can affect groups differently. Their distributional effects depend on which taxes change, who receives spending or transfers, and how programmes are implemented.

## India's Union Budget

The Union Budget presents estimates and policy proposals for the central government, including receipts, expenditure, and financing. State governments also publish their own budgets. Budget documents distinguish estimates from actual results and provide supporting detail; current-year amounts and measures should be checked in the latest official documents.
