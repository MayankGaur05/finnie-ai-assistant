# Tax Laws in India: An Overview

## Summary

Tax laws define when a tax applies, who is responsible for it, how it is calculated and paid, and what reporting or record-keeping is required. India's tax framework includes laws at different levels of government and for different kinds of tax. Which rules apply depends on the transaction, taxpayer, location, and tax period.

- **Acts and other legislation** establish the main legal framework for a tax. Examples include laws dealing with income tax, goods and services tax, and customs duties.
- **Rules and prescribed forms** provide procedures and details for applying legislation, such as reporting information or submitting returns.
- **Finance Acts and other amendments** can change tax provisions. Budgets may propose changes, but the enacted law and effective dates determine what applies.
- **Notifications and circulars** issued by the relevant authorities may communicate commencement dates, procedures, clarifications, or administrative instructions. Their effect depends on the law and the issuing authority.
- **Court decisions** interpret legal provisions and can affect how a rule is understood and applied.
- Tax requirements may differ between central, state, and local authorities. A rule that applies to one tax or state may not apply to another.
- Tax law is time-sensitive. Check the official authority's current information for the relevant tax period instead of relying on an old guide, tax rate, return form, or deadline.

## India's income-tax law transition

The Income-tax Act, 2025 took effect on April 1, 2026, replacing the Income-tax Act, 1961 for tax years beginning on or after that date. Relevant earlier tax years and transitional matters may continue under the 1961 Act. The applicable act and terminology therefore depend on the period being considered.

## Limitation

This article is a general overview, not legal, tax, or filing advice. It does not identify the rules or deadlines that apply to an individual or business. Tax provisions and administrative guidance change; consult current official sources or a qualified tax professional for a specific situation.
