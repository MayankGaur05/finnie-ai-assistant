# Charitable Donations and Possible Tax Deductions in India

## Summary

A charitable donation is a voluntary contribution of money or other support to a charitable cause or organisation. A donation and a tax deduction are different things: making a donation does not by itself guarantee a tax benefit. Any deduction depends on the applicable law for the tax period, the donor's circumstances, the recipient's eligibility, and the conditions attached to the contribution.

- Check whether the recipient organisation or fund is recognised or approved for the relevant tax treatment. Do not assume every charity qualifies.
- Keep the donation receipt and related records, such as the recipient's name, date, amount, and any identification or approval details shown on the receipt.
- Tax rules may treat cash, electronic transfers, donations in kind, and other payment methods differently. Confirm the rules that apply before claiming a deduction.
- A deduction, when permitted, generally changes the income on which tax is calculated; it is not the same as receiving the full donation amount back as a refund.
- The ClearTax article describes the former Section 80G framework under the Income-tax Act, 1961. India's Income-tax Act, 2025 took effect on April 1, 2026, and contains current donation-deduction provisions. Rules for earlier tax periods and transition cases may still depend on the 1961 Act.
- The current official law includes conditions relating to eligible funds or institutions, payment methods, documentation, and limits. Check those details for the relevant tax year rather than applying percentages or limits from an older summary.

## Limitation

This article is general education, not tax or legal advice. It does not confirm that a specific donation qualifies or calculate a deduction. Tax treatment depends on current law, the applicable tax period, the recipient's status, the payment method, and other conditions. Verify current requirements with the Income Tax Department or a qualified tax professional.
